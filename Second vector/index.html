Executive Summary

A Low-severity Entra ID Protection alert on finance user m.smith@lognpacific.org proved to be the visible edge of a complete Business Email Compromise. A stolen session cookie was replayed through Outlook Web to bypass MFA, the operator profiled the account via Graph API, planted concealment and forwarding inbox rules, exfiltrated three sensitive files from SharePoint, and ran an internal payment-redirect scam against a colleague — reinforced minutes later on Teams.

A Power Automate flow planted during the session kept forwarding mail to an external Proton address nine hours after the human operator logged off. Conditional Access reported notApplied on every successful sign-in; the tenant's MFA policy never engaged on the attacker's traffic.

No malware, no endpoint, no outage. Every action read as routine in isolation. Risk assessment is Critical despite the original Low rating.

Three Rhythms

The compromise has three rhythms. Reading them in order is what turns a pile of low-severity events into a single coherent intrusion.

Rhythm 01 · Silent
Cookie Birth
May 31, 2026
Password change at 00:53:27 coincides with first connection from the attacker's /24. Legitimate user almost certainly authenticated through an AiTM phishing kit; the session cookie was harvested at the proxy.
Rhythm 02 · Human
The Operator
Jun 11 · 02:22 – 04:18 UTC
Two bad-password attempts via legacy Office client, then Graph recon, then a single OWA sign-in marked singleFactorAuthentication. Inside one session: two inbox rules, three SharePoint downloads, fraud email, Teams reinforcement.
Rhythm 03 · Robotic
The Flow Wakes
Jun 11 · 12:41 PM UTC
Power Automate calls Graph /forward from a Microsoft-owned Azure IP. The attacker is offline; their automation is not. The OAuth grant on AppId 7ab7862c holds the door open until explicitly revoked.

Every individual signal was unremarkable. The evidence of intent lives only in the pattern — and the pattern only assembles when seven log sources are tied to one session GUID.

Key Findings

F-01

Token Replay Bypassed MFA Entirely

29 successful sign-ins from the attacker's IP, every one tagged singleFactorAuthentication with AuthenticationMethod: Previously satisfied. The session cookie claimed prior MFA without ever prompting — the AiTM signature on an MFA-required tenant.

Critical
F-02

Conditional Access Never Engaged

ConditionalAccessStatus = notApplied on all 38 successful sign-ins. The policy exists but did not cover the legacy authentication path the attacker used. This is the largest single control failure of the incident.

Critical
F-03

Pre-Login Graph Recon Profiled MFA Posture

Three Graph API calls to /userRegistrationDetails against m.smith in the six minutes before the successful OWA login. The attacker checked exactly what they were about to bypass before spending the token.

High
F-04

Two Inbox Rules, Nine Seconds Apart

Invoice Processing moved replies to Archive (concealment, not deletion — deletion would create a gap). Backup Copy forwarded inbound mail to merovingian1337@proton.me. Together: the victim sees nothing; the attacker sees everything.

Critical
F-05

Power Automate Flow Survives Password Reset

OAuth grant on AppId 7ab7862c-4c57-491e-8a45-d52a7e023983 kept calling Graph /forward nine hours after the human session ended — from a Microsoft Azure IP, not the attacker's. Persistence at the cloud-identity layer, not the endpoint.

Critical
F-06

Three SharePoint Files Taken in One Second

VPN credentials, vendor banking details, and a workbook — downloaded at 03:37 AM from a Linux UA on an anonymous proxy. A curated pull (one for re-entry, one for fraud, one for reference), not a smash-and-grab.

Critical
F-07

Multi-Channel Internal BEC

Fraudulent email to j.reynolds at 04:13:54 ("Updated Banking Details"), reinforced by a Teams message four minutes later on the same theme. Pretext mined from a real Feb 8 vendor-payment thread inside the mailbox.

Critical
F-08

Exfil Address Was a Known Contact

merovingian1337@proton.me corresponded with m.smith on Feb 8 ("Updated Invoice - Meridian Consulting Q1 2026") — four months before the active intrusion. Either a vendor whose trust was abused, or evidence of prior reconnaissance.

Attack Phase Analysis

Each phase opens with the analytic question that drove it. The thread that held everything together was the session GUID 005d431a-380b-1f5e-e554-16d5010dc28e — present in SigninLogs.SessionId and embedded in CloudAppEvents.RawEventData.

PHASE 1 Initial Access — The Replayed Cookie T1550.004 ›

29 successful sign-ins from 103.69.224.136, every one tagged AuthenticationRequirement = singleFactorAuthentication. AuthenticationDetails shows "authenticationMethod":"Previously satisfied" — Entra's way of saying "I'm not asking for MFA again because this session already proved it elsewhere." A session token does not re-authenticate a person; it claims a person already did, and Entra trusts the claim.

Two failed password attempts at 02:22 and 02:24 (ResultType 50126 via legacy "Microsoft Office" app) preceded the OWA token replay by ~50 minutes. The sequence is the attacker's logic: try the password against a legacy endpoint, fail, then commit the stolen cookie against the modern web client where it cannot be challenged.

SigninLogs
| where IPAddress == "103.69.224.136"
| where ResultType == 0
| extend AuthDetails = tostring(AuthenticationDetails)
| project TimeGenerated, UserPrincipalName, AppDisplayName,
          AuthenticationRequirement, AuthDetails, SessionId
| sort by TimeGenerated asc
PHASE 2 Discovery — Profiling Before Striking T1087.004 · T1069.003 ›

Six minutes before the successful sign-in, three Graph calls to /userRegistrationDetails against m.smith. Same endpoint admins use to audit MFA registration; calling it from an end-user context is textbook account discovery. A separate call to /rolemanagement/directory/transitiveRoleAssignments asked the privilege question ("am I admin?"). At 03:45, a different AppId queried /me/memberOf — the social graph that becomes the BEC target list.

The ordering is the signal: failed-passwords → MFA-posture recon → role recon → token replay → group enumeration → file access → mail send. No log source seen in isolation will catch this; only cross-table correlation by session ID will.

MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs
| where TimeGenerated between (datetime(2026-06-11 03:00) .. datetime(2026-06-11 04:00))
| where RequestUri contains "userRegistrationDetails"
| where RequestUri contains "m.smith"
| project TimeGenerated, IPAddress, RequestUri, ResponseStatusCode
PHASE 3 Persistence — Inbox Rules and a Sleeping Flow T1564.008 · T1546 ›

Stage one: two inbox rules created nine seconds apart. Invoice Processing moves replies to Archive (not Delete — deletion creates a noticeable gap; an Archive move keeps mail readable by the attacker and invisible to the victim). Backup Copy forwards to merovingian1337@proton.me. The naming is camouflage.

Stage two: a Power Automate flow created at ~03:28 produces an OAuth grant against AppId 7ab7862c-4c57-491e-8a45-d52a7e023983. That grant is the durable artifact. A password reset doesn't revoke it. Disabling the account doesn't revoke it. Revoking sessions doesn't revoke it. Only Enterprise Applications → that AppId → Remove does.

OfficeActivity
| where Operation in ("New-InboxRule", "Set-InboxRule")
| where UserId == "m.smith@lognpacific.org"
| project TimeGenerated, ClientIP, Operation, Parameters
PHASE 4 Internal Phish — The Con T1534 ›

At 04:13:54, an email left m.smith's mailbox to j.reynolds — subject "Updated Banking Details - Pacific IT Monthly". Real sender, real signature, real history. Not a spoof. Pretext mined four minutes earlier from a real Feb 8 vendor-payment thread inside the same mailbox. Classic BEC playbook: read first, write second.

Four minutes later, a Teams MessageSent from the same session reinforced the email. Two channels means two trust signals and one social-pressure cue. The Invoice Processing rule guarantees the reply gets archived; the Backup Copy rule guarantees the attacker sees the reply anyway; the Teams message guarantees the recipient is in a hurry.

EmailEvents
| where SenderFromAddress == "m.smith@lognpacific.org"
| where SenderIPv4 == "103.69.224.136"
| project TimeGenerated, Subject, RecipientEmailAddress, DeliveryAction
PHASE 5 Exfiltration — Two Hands on the Files T1530 · T1020 ›

Hand one — manual SharePoint downloads at 03:37: VPN credentials, vendor banking sheet, a workbook. Three files in one second is a curated pull. FileDownloaded is categorically different from FileAccessed: it produces a copy off-tenant. yomark.pdf was viewed but not downloaded — pointer file pattern (T1555).

Hand two — autonomous Power Automate forwarding at 12:41 PM. The cause lives in MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs (the Graph /forward call from AppId 7ab7862c on Azure IP 20.150.129.194); the effect lives in EmailEvents (the FW: copy to merovingian1337@proton.me five seconds later). The attacker's IP is completely absent. No human, no keyboard — just Microsoft's own infrastructure executing the planted automation.

MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs
| where AppId == "7ab7862c-4c57-491e-8a45-d52a7e023983"
| where RequestUri has_any ("/forward", "/sendMail")
| project TimeGenerated, IPAddress, RequestUri, ResponseStatusCode

Response & Recommendations

Containment must follow the listed order. Password reset alone leaves the flow running; rule deletion alone leaves the flow running. Only OAuth-grant revocation closes the durable channel.

🔴 Containment Order (sequential)

  • Revoke m.smith's active sign-in sessions (kills the cookie)
  • Reset password & force MFA re-registration
  • Revoke OAuth grant on AppId 7ab7862c in Enterprise Applications
  • Delete the Power Automate flow in admin.powerplatform.microsoft.com
  • Delete inbox rules "Invoice Processing" and "Backup Copy"
  • Rotate every credential in the downloaded files
  • Notify j.reynolds out-of-band (not email)
  • Mailbox audit: all sent items between 03:15 – 04:18 UTC
  • Investigate merovingian1337@proton.me as pre-existing contact

🛡️ Detection Engineering

  • Correlate SigninLogs.SessionId across CloudAppEvents, OfficeActivity, EmailEvents, Graph
  • Alert on every New-InboxRule with external ForwardTo/RedirectTo
  • Alert on MoveToFolder rules with sender/subject filters
  • Daily OAuth-grant review for finance, HR, exec users
  • Alert: singleFactorAuthentication + ConditionalAccessStatus=notApplied
  • Alert: Graph /forward from non-Microsoft AppId > 5×/24h
  • Alert: /userRegistrationDetails from end-user contexts

🟠 Policy & Hardening

  • Block legacy authentication via Conditional Access
  • Require phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2 / Windows Hello) for finance & approvers
  • Treat singleFactorAuthentication on anonymized IPs as a CA trigger
  • Restrict Power Automate connector creation for non-admin users
  • Require admin approval for OAuth consents on sensitive scopes
  • Reduce session token lifetimes for finance/approver roles

🔵 Detection Gaps Observed

  • ConditionalAccessStatus=notApplied on 38/38 successes — policy didn't cover the path
  • 7 of 8 risk events auto-dismissed as Low
  • External-forward inbox rules not alerting
  • OAuth grants to non-Microsoft AppIds not surfaced
  • No cross-table session-GUID correlation in place
  • Legacy "Microsoft Office" auth surface still reachable
  • AuditLogs returned zero records — coverage gap
09

Final Assessment

The night-shift verdict — Low severity, dismissed — was wrong. The compromise scored Low because every individual signal scored Low: one anonymous sign-in, one inbox rule, a handful of SharePoint downloads, an internal email between colleagues. The compromise lives entirely in the pattern, and the pattern only assembles when seven log sources are bound to one session GUID.

On attacker sophistication. The operator is deliberate, not opportunistic. ~45 minute pause between failed passwords and the cookie commit, Graph reconnaissance against the account's MFA posture, pretext mined from a four-month-old vendor thread, concealment and forwarding planted nine seconds apart, and a Power Automate flow that survives every credential-level remediation. This is the BEC tradecraft of an operator who has done this before.

On defensive posture. The tenant has the telemetry to reconstruct this attack — every record needed for this report was present in Sentinel. What it does not yet have is the analytic surface to surface it in real time. The single most impactful change is correlation by session GUID across SigninLogs, CloudAppEvents, OfficeActivity, EmailEvents, and MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs. The second most impactful is OAuth-grant review for finance, HR, and exec users.

On risk rating. The compromise is Critical, not Low. Three sensitive files left the tenant. A BEC was executed against an internal colleague with social-pressure reinforcement on a second channel. The exfiltration mechanism continues to run autonomously until an OAuth grant is explicitly revoked. Breach-notification obligations should be assessed in the next 24 hours.
